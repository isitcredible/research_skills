# Pre-analysis plans — Olken (2015)

Working distillation of Benjamin A. Olken, "Promises and Perils of
Pre-Analysis Plans," *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 29(3): 61–80.
Verified against the published article (JEP furniture on page 1; source
PDF not distributed).

## What a PAP pins down (Table 1 checklist, pp. 64–65)

Primary outcome variable (precise variable definition, not topic);
specification and estimation method; covariates; subgroup analyses;
sample definition and exclusion rules; standard-error construction;
multiple-testing corrections. Olken draws the list from FDA clinical-
trial practice and notes where social science departs from it.

## The perils (the review-relevant half)

- A PAP is only as strong as its precision: vague variable definitions
  or "and related outcomes" language leaves the flexibility in place.
- Deviation is not inherently damning — the informative exercise is the
  *diff*: what was prespecified vs what was reported, and whether the
  deviations move the results toward significance.
- PAPs cannot anticipate everything; the honest treatment is the PAP
  analysis alongside the exploratory analysis, labelled as such — not
  silence about which is which.

## Uses in the review skill

- `paper` PAP/preregistration item: a PAP is a dated prior
  version of the analysis; the version-history machinery (what changed,
  when, and in which direction relative to the claims) applies directly.
  Table 1's checklist defines the dimensions the diff enumerates.
- `experiment-diagnostics` outcome-family check: Olken's primary-
  outcome discipline is the standard the prespecified-outcome test
  applies.
