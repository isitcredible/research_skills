# Anomaly-test applicability

Anomaly tests localize questions. They do not establish intent.

## Benford or first-digit tests

Use only when the variable:

- is generated by a process compatible with Benford-like scaling;
- spans several orders of magnitude;
- is not assigned, bounded, truncated, floored, or dominated by zeros;
- has enough observations for the intended test.

Report the eligibility filter and all variables tested. Do not select one
visually suspicious series after inspecting many and report an unadjusted
p-value.

At large N, χ² and KS conformity tests reject for trivial deviations in
genuine data. Measure conformity by effect size — Nigrini's mean absolute
deviation (MAD) — against comparable genuine data; a significance test
alone is not a flag.

## Terminal digits and heaping

Use only on raw recorded values whose range spans many last-digit units,
where the source claims measurements precise enough that terminal digits
should vary — never on computed, converted, or aggregated values, whose
last digits need not be uniform (Mosimann et al. 1995). Compare by source,
coder, wave, and measurement regime.

Round-number heaping is often normal in historical or survey data. Its absence
can be as informative as its presence when hand collection is claimed.

## Arithmetic feasibility

Use GRIM/GRIMMER-style or accounting-identity checks only when the reported
sample size, rounding convention, weighting, denominator, and statistic
definition are known. GRIM needs integer-valued items or a known
granularity (for a k-item scale the grain is 1/(N·k)), and the grain must
be coarser than the reporting precision — N·k below 10^(reported
decimals), N alone for one integer item; otherwise every reported mean is
attainable.
Weighted or adjusted summaries can invalidate a naive integer-feasibility
check.

## Excess similarity and duplicates

Test:

- exact duplicated rows;
- long identical sequences across independent units;
- repeated residual or decimal patterns;
- implausibly low between-unit variance;
- balance or similarity beyond the assignment process.

First exclude shared source lineage, deterministic transforms, copying during
legitimate imputation, and code-level joins.

## Batch, coder, wave, and seam breaks

Compare distributions and residual patterns across known construction batches.
Predeclare the seam or adjust for searching across many possible breakpoints.
Link every detected break back to the chain map.

## Reporting a flag

Each flag must state:

- test and applicability;
- population and sample;
- null/reference distribution;
- multiplicity treatment;
- statistic and uncertainty;
- benign explanations tested;
- next source or code check;
- whether the headline result moves after correction.
